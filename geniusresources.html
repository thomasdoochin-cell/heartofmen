OneFinding Your Genius Prompts

I’d encourage you to do these as individual reflection sessions. When we sit down to journal, we begin to stir something up in our subconscious. This needs some time to work in us to reveal what we need. We’re not trying to “define our genius” in these prompts. We are looking to come into deeper relationship. It takes time; let it work on you.

Prompt 1: Bridging worlds

What two worlds are you upholding that you’ve always believed to be separate? (maybe you’ve always felt split between them). What would happen if they came together at one confluence? Who would you be? What story would have to break down if these worlds bridged?

Prompt 2: As a kid

What did you love as a child? If you could choose any scene to go inhabit and play within, what would it be? What did you want to be when you grew up? Ask your parents or older siblings when they remembered you to be the most shining and joyful. Notice the words they use.

Prompt 3: Most vibrant moments

In the last few decades of your life, tell me about the moments where you felt the most you. Invigorated, joyful, fulfilled. Those moments that have nothing to do with what someone else thought or what you meant to them. What are the common threads across these moments?

Prompt 4: Your strongest stories

What are the strongest stories/judgements you have about the way life just is? (something that feels limiting). How may you have justified the abandoning of your own genius? What is the opposite story of the one you most frequently tell? Who would you be in that opposite story?
